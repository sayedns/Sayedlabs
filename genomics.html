Functional Cardiovascular Genomics

From genetic association to experimentally tested mechanisms.

We pair human iPSC disease models with genome engineering, functional screens, and single-cell multi-omics to investigate how genetic variants and regulatory programs change cardiovascular cell behavior.

Explore our questions →← All research
Our perspective

Why this matters

A disease-associated genetic variant does not always reveal how disease develops. We investigate whether a variant changes a cardiovascular phenotype, which regulatory pathways it affects, and whether perturbing these pathways alters the outcome. Our approach integrates genome editing and molecular profiling with functional cardiovascular assays.

Central question

Which genetic variants and regulatory networks causally influence cardiovascular disease, and how can their effects be tested in human models?

Research directions

What we investigate

01

Variant-to-function analysis

Introduce or correct candidate variants in defined genetic backgrounds to test their effects on relevant cardiovascular lineages.

02

CRISPR-based functional screening

Use targeted perturbations and scalable CRISPRi/a strategies to prioritize disease-associated and therapeutically relevant genes.

03

Single-cell regulatory landscapes

Combine transcriptional and chromatin information to identify cell states, regulatory networks, and intercellular signaling programs.

Experimental strategy

From observation to mechanism

01

Genetic candidates

Prioritize disease variants, genes, and regulatory elements from human datasets.

02

Isogenic human cells

Generate and compare genetically defined iPSC-derived cardiovascular lineages.

03

Multi-omic analysis

Measure transcriptional and chromatin changes alongside cellular function.

04

Functional validation

Test candidate regulatory mechanisms and their physiological consequences.
