Cardiomyopathy & Heart Failure

The failing heart is a multicellular system.

We study how endothelial cells, cardiomyocytes, fibroblasts, and their surrounding environment communicate in inherited and acquired cardiac disease. Patient-derived models allow us to test how disrupted vascular–myocardial signaling shapes cardiac function.

Explore our questions →← All research
Our perspective

Why this matters

Cardiomyopathy is often studied as a disorder within cardiomyocytes themselves. Our published LMNA work demonstrates that dysfunction in the cardiac endothelium can impair cardiomyocyte function and contribute to disease. By investigating the signaling between vascular and muscle cells, we aim to identify disease mechanisms that would be difficult to detect in isolated cardiomyocytes.

Central question

How do pathological endothelial states influence cardiomyocyte function, and which pathways might restore healthy vascular–myocardial communication?

Research directions

What we investigate

01

Vascular–myocardial crosstalk

Define the signals that connect endothelial injury to cardiomyocyte dysfunction, contractile changes, and tissue remodeling.

02

Genetic cardiomyopathy

Use patient-derived and genome-engineered models to study LMNA-associated disease and other inherited cardiomyopathies.

03

Endothelial remodeling and fibrosis

Investigate endothelial plasticity, EndoMT-associated programs, and interactions with fibroblasts during pathological remodeling.

Experimental strategy

From observation to mechanism

01

Patient biology

Connect patient-derived cardiac and vascular samples with clinically relevant disease phenotypes.

02

Human cell models

Compare donor-matched or genetically defined iPSC cardiomyocytes and endothelial cells.

03

Multicellular function

Test intercellular communication and tissue-level effects in engineered cardiac systems.

04

Causal pathways

Use molecular profiling, genome perturbation, and rescue studies to evaluate candidate mechanisms.
