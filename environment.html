Environmental Cardiovascular Disease

Investigating the cardiovascular effects of exposures and biological context.

We develop human cardiovascular models to study how environmental stressors interact with genetic background and influence endothelial, inflammatory, and myocardial responses.

Explore our questions →← All research
Our perspective

Why this matters

Environmental exposures may trigger cellular stress responses whose consequences vary with biological context. We use defined experimental exposures and human cardiovascular models to ask how vascular injury, inflammatory signaling, and multicellular communication change after exposure. This program focuses on mechanistic questions; ongoing studies are not presented here as established findings.

Central question

How do exposures and prior biological conditions influence cardiovascular responses, and which changes could prove reversible?

Research directions

What we investigate

01

Exposure-associated vascular injury

Measure endothelial function, inflammation, and cellular stress following defined environmental challenges.

02

Gene–environment interactions

Compare genetically diverse or matched human iPSC-derived cardiovascular cells to study variation in susceptibility.

03

Persistent and multicellular responses

Investigate recovery, remodeling, and endothelial–cardiomyocyte interactions in engineered tissue models.

Experimental strategy

From observation to mechanism

01

Defined exposure design

Establish controlled exposure and recovery conditions in human models.

02

Multicellular human platforms

Study endothelial cells, cardiomyocytes, and engineered cardiovascular tissues.

03

Molecular and functional readouts

Integrate vascular and cardiac phenotyping with transcriptional and epigenomic profiling.

04

Mechanistic follow-up

Test whether prioritized pathways alter injury or recovery responses.
