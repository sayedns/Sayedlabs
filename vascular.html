Vascular Biology & Regeneration

Understanding the endothelium as a driver of disease and repair.

We investigate how endothelial cells sense injury, change their identity, communicate with neighboring cells, and contribute to vascular dysfunction or recovery.

Explore our questions →← All research
Our perspective

Why this matters

Healthy vascular function depends on more than an intact endothelial barrier. Endothelial cells integrate mechanical, inflammatory, and metabolic signals, and their responses can shape tissue remodeling. Our research examines disease-associated endothelial states—including endothelial-to-mesenchymal programs—and asks when such changes are reversible.

Central question

Which endothelial programs contribute to vascular injury, and how can human models reveal opportunities for vascular repair?

Research directions

What we investigate

01

Endothelial dysfunction and plasticity

Identify inflammatory, epigenetic, and mechanosensitive pathways that alter endothelial cell identity and function.

02

Inherited and rare vascular disease

Investigate patient-specific vascular phenotypes using iPSC-derived endothelial and smooth muscle cells, including models of genetic aortopathies.

03

Vascular repair and regeneration

Study how endothelial states, cell-cell interactions, and regulatory programs may be redirected toward healthier vascular function.

Experimental strategy

From observation to mechanism

01

Patient tissue and biobanking

Anchor experimental questions in vascular disease specimens and clinical observations.

02

Human vascular models

Differentiate patient-derived iPSCs into endothelial cells and vascular smooth muscle cells.

03

State and function

Integrate vascular functional assays with single-cell and epigenomic profiling.

04

Mechanism and intervention

Test candidate endothelial pathways using targeted perturbation and rescue experiments.
