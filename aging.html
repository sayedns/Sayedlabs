Aging & Cardiovascular Disease

Understanding biological age, vascular decline, and cardiovascular resilience.

We study how inflammatory and cellular changes associated with aging relate to vascular integrity, cardiac function, and responses to stress.

Explore our questions →← All research
Our perspective

Why this matters

Chronological age does not fully describe cardiovascular aging. Our published work on the inflammatory aging clock (iAge) connected immune-associated molecular signatures with age-related health outcomes, including cardiovascular aging. Building on this broader framework, we examine how inflammation, endothelial states, and cardiometabolic stress may contribute to variation in cardiovascular resilience.

Central question

Which molecular signatures distinguish cardiovascular aging from chronological age, and which biological processes could be targeted to preserve function?

Research directions

What we investigate

01

Inflammatory biological age

Investigate molecular and immune-related signatures associated with biological aging and cardiovascular risk.

02

Vascular and endothelial aging

Study how endothelial dysfunction, inflammation, and tissue remodeling change over the lifespan.

03

Resilience and susceptibility

Explore how aging may interact with genetic background and environmental stressors to influence cardiovascular responses.

Experimental strategy

From observation to mechanism

01

Human signatures

Relate molecular and clinical information to age-associated cardiovascular phenotypes.

02

Computational integration

Identify candidate biological age signatures and associated pathways.

03

Human cardiovascular models

Investigate age-related cellular programs in experimentally tractable systems.

04

Mechanistic testing

Prioritize and test candidate regulators of vascular and myocardial stress responses.
